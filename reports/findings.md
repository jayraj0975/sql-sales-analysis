# Findings

Generated by `src/run_analysis.py`. Every number below is computed from the query results.

**About the data.** Chinook is a *sample* database for a fictional digital music store
(2021 to 2025, 59 customers). It is built for teaching SQL, and its sales are very
evenly spread, so several results below are "flat" or "even" rather than dramatic. The value
of this project is the SQL and its checks, not a discovery about a real business.

## What the queries show

1. **Revenue is flat.** Yearly revenue stays between $449 and $481; year-over-year
   changes run from -5.6% to +7.1%. There is no growth trend to explain. In 43 of
   60 months revenue is *exactly* $37.62, which is the signature of generated data, not of a real store.
2. **No small group of customers dominates revenue.** It takes 29 customers to reach 50% of revenue and
   47 of 59 to reach 80%; the top 10 customers hold 19.4%.
3. **USA is the largest market** at 22.5% of revenue, and the top three countries together
   are 43.9%. Among the five largest countries revenue per customer is $37.99 to $40.24,
   so their revenue mostly follows how many customers they have. Across all 24 countries it ranges from
   $37.62 to $46.62, but 15 countries have a single customer, so the small-country figures are noisy.
4. **Genre revenue is concentrated.** Rock earns 35.5% and the top three of 24 genres
   earn 63.1%.
5. **43.4% of the catalogue never sold** (1,519 of 3,503 tracks). The data has no cost
   information, so what that inventory costs to carry cannot be assessed here.
6. **Most customers keep buying.** Of the 46 customers whose first purchase was in 2021,
   72% were active in 2022, 80% were active in 2023, 78% were active in 2024, 72% were active in 2025.
   ("Active" means at least one invoice that year, so a customer can skip a year and return.)
7. **RFM segments:** 29 middle, 26 low value, 4 high value customers. 58 of 59 customers have exactly
   7 invoices, so the Frequency score separates them almost not at all and the segments are really Recency plus Monetary;
   treat them as illustrative.

## What this analysis cannot tell you

- The data is synthetic-style sample data, so patterns here do not describe any real store.
- There are no costs, margins, discounts or refunds, so this is revenue, not profit.
- Five years and 59 customers is too little for statistical claims; differences of a few percent are noise.
- RFM bands here are illustrative for the reason in point 7.
